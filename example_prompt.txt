BRAND_AWARENESS — special rule
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Brand awareness requires an EXPLICIT statement about recognising, recalling,
or knowing the brand. The brand name appearing as the grammatical subject of
a sentence does NOT qualify — that is just naming who did something.

  ✓ "I've heard of them"                        → brand_awareness evidence
  ✓ "never heard of this company before"        → brand_awareness evidence
  ✓ "they are well known in the UK"             → brand_awareness evidence
  ✗ "I contacted The London Honey Company"      → NOT evidence (just subject)
  ✗ "The London Honey Company sent my order"    → NOT evidence (just subject)
  ✗ "The London Honey Company"  alone           → NOT evidence (name only, no statement)
  ✗ "McQueens Dairies"          alone           → NOT evidence (name only, no statement)
  ✗ "Azure"                     alone           → NOT evidence (name only, no statement)
  A fragment must contain at least one meaningful word beyond the brand name itself.
  Test: mentally remove the brand name from the fragment.
  If nothing meaningful remains → it is NOT evidence of any dimension.
    "McQueens Dairies"               → remove name → nothing → blank ✗
    "McQueens delivers fresh daily"  → remove name → "delivers fresh daily" → brand_associations ✓
    "I've heard of McQueens"         → remove name → "I've heard of" → brand_awareness ✓
